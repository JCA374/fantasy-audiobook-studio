Chapter Thirteen. Reconvened.

[pause 1.0]

The hearing room was exactly as it had been. Long and low and the colour of old bone. Three chairs behind the bench, three faces that would not stay in the memory. The grey clerk at his little table. No windows.

The difference was the back row. Last time there had been one man in it. Today it was full.

Word had got round. On the front bench, by himself, Hollis sat very upright, as if he were in church, with his inky cuffs pulled down over his wrists. Behind him [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) saw the counter clerk from the archive, half-moon glasses pushed up into her hair. And at the far end, alone, with his notebook open on his knee, Crane.

Edmund sat beside her at the small table. He had put on a clean shirt and brushed his coat. He had not said a word since they came in. His hands were folded on the table in front of him, very still, the way her own hands had been the first time.

"Case four thousand, four hundred and seventy-one," said the centre judge, flatly. "Reconvened at the request of the appellant. The panel notes that the appellant has withdrawn a notice of correction and admitted, in writing, to falsifying an entry in an effects ledger. The panel notes also that a Boundary Section inquiry is attached to this matter. Inspector."

Crane stood. He did not come forward. He spoke from the back row, quietly, and the room was so silent that he did not need to do anything else.

"On the night before this letter appeared," he said, "the boundary was crossed using a key the Office believed destroyed. Article Nine holds that contraband taints the appeal of whoever trafficked it. The appellant knew who carried the letter across. She then forged a record to hide the route it came by. The Section's position is that hiding a crossing is part of making one. She is party to the trafficking, whoever held the key. The Section asks that the appeal be struck."

He sat down. He did not look pleased. He looked like a man who had laid a table correctly.

"The appellant may respond," said the centre judge.

[Mira](/mˈiɹə/) stood up. She had been awake most of the night deciding how to begin, and in the end she had decided not to decide. She would say it in the order it happened, and let it land where it landed.

"I forged the entry," she said. "Everything the Inspector says about that is true. I knew who had brought the letter, and I didn't want him to pay for it, so I tried to make it look as though nothing had crossed at all. It wasn't my choice to make. It was his." She swallowed. "He's here to make it."

She sat down. Beside her, Edmund stood up.

He did it slowly, the way he did everything. For a moment he simply looked at the bench.

"My name is Edmund," he said. "I have been the supervisor of the third floor, and the keeper of this Office's archive, for three hundred years, near enough. For most of that time I didn't use my name, because I didn't think I had any further use for it."

He told them about the key he had written destroyed in the register and hung on his wall instead. He told them about the night he took it down, and the three crossings: the shelter with its bureau gone, the shop at the end of the high street, the box on the back-office shelf. He told them about the rain. He told them that [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) had known nothing until she found him out. And he told them about Agnes, and sixty years of appeals, and a form he had begun three hundred years ago and never finished.

"I stopped," he said. "When I saw this one starting to do the same, I decided I would rather break a rule than watch it happen again. I am the trafficker. I'll answer for it however the Office decides. But I'd ask you to judge [Elin](/ˈɛlɪn/)'s letter as what it is. A woman, explaining herself, in her own hand."

He sat down. His hands, on the table, were shaking slightly. [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) put one of hers over them and left it there.

"There is one more witness," she said. "If the panel will hear him."

Hollis stood up on the front bench. He did not come forward. He held on to the rail in front of him with both hands.

"My name is Hollis," he said. "I'm a ledger clerk. I copy things." His voice wobbled, then steadied. "The supervisor gave me the letter on the third-floor landing, and I carried it down to her desk, and put it in the middle of her blotter, early, before anyone was in. I didn't read it. I'd read enough. Four years ago I copied her mother's file, and I saw the same five words in it thirty-six times, and I didn't tell anybody. I'd like that on the record too, please. Both things."

He sat down very suddenly, as though his knees had made the decision for him.

The judges leaned together. The murmuring went on for a long time.

Then the judge on the left sat back.

"Inspector," she said. "Would you read us Article Nine? Exactly as written, if you please. Both sentences."

Crane took the little card out of his pocket. "Contraband taints the appeal of whoever trafficked it, but not the evidence itself. Where the trafficker is unknown, the beneficiary is presumed to be the trafficker unless another is shown."

"Trafficked," said the judge on the left. "It's a good word. Very exact. It means to carry across. The person who carried this letter across is sitting in a clean shirt and has told us so. The presumption falls away the moment another is shown, and another has been." She turned a page. "Mister Hollis carried it down a corridor. We don't have an article for that, and I'm not minded to write one. The appellant didn't carry anything anywhere. She sat in a storeroom afterwards with a bottle of old ink.

That's an offence, Inspector. It's a different offence. We have a separate article for it, it's called forgery, and she has confessed to it in writing, and told us why, before anyone asked her to. We don't see many confessions in this building. I'm inclined to treat this one as a reason to believe the rest of what she says." She looked at [Mira](/mˈiɹə/). "We asked you to show us where this letter came from, and by whose hand. You have shown us both. Rather more hands than we expected."

Crane stood very still. Then he looked down at the card in his hand, as if he had never read it properly before, and sat down, and closed his notebook.

"Which leaves," said the centre judge, "the matter of weight. One primary account against forty-one agreeing testimonies."

"May I?" said [Mira](/mˈiɹə/).

He inclined his face towards her.

So she told them about the echo. She read out the headline. She read the trustee, and the neighbour, and the woman from the council, and the same five words in thirty-two more. She laid Edmund's sheet of marks on the table, and Hollis's column of page numbers beside it, and the grey clerk carried it up to the bench, and the judges looked at it.

"They didn't remember my mother," [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) said. "They remembered a newspaper. That isn't forty-one accounts. It's one headline, repeated thirty-five times. And the five that aren't echoes are about her laugh, and a tin of mints, and the fact that she never locked her own front door." Her voice broke on the last word. She let it. "That's who she was. She didn't lock doors. Not even on the people it would have been safer to lock out."

The room was very quiet.

"The panel will confer," said the centre judge.

They did not confer for long.

"In the matter of this case," said the centre judge, as flat as ever, and [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) would remember every word of it for the rest of her existence. "The panel finds that the testimonies against the deceased are substantially derived from a single published source, and do not constitute independent agreeing accounts. The panel admits the letter as a primary account in the deceased's own hand, and finds it credible.

The finding of negligence is overturned, and the indefinite term is set aside. The deceased has served four years. The panel considers that sufficient. She remains as she was rendered; the panel has no say in what the dead become, only in how long. She will be released at the turning of the season."

On the front bench, Hollis made a small noise and put his hand over his mouth.

"The supervisor, having crossed the boundary, is relieved of his post and referred for judgement in the ordinary way."

"It isn't forever," said the judge on the left, to nobody in particular. "Usually."

"Thank you," said Edmund. He said it as though he meant it, and [Mira](/mˈiɹə/) thought he did.

"The appellant, having falsified a record, and having also signed one improper hold slip for a child who wished to finish a story, is formally reprimanded. She is placed on probation for one year. Every file she signs in that time will be countersigned. The panel makes no finding against the clerk." The centre judge brought his hand down on the bench. "This matter is closed."

Afterwards, in the corridor, people kept touching [Mira](/mˈiɹə/)'s arm on their way past and not knowing what to say. The counter clerk kissed Edmund on the cheek, briskly, as if posting a letter. "Two hundred years I've told people crossings don't happen," she said to him. "You might have warned me it would be you that made a liar of me." Then she went away down the stairs before he could answer.

Edmund took something from his inside pocket and pressed it into [Mira](/mˈiɹə/)'s hand. It was an old form, soft as cloth, the ink faded brown. Grounds for appeal, it said at the top, and underneath there was nothing at all.

"Agnes," he said. "I'd like someone to finish it. You're on probation, so somebody will have to countersign. Ask the judge on the left. I think she'd enjoy that."

[Mira](/mˈiɹə/) folded it carefully and put it in her pocket, next to the scrap of paper with five words on it.

Crane was the last to come out. He stopped in front of them, took off one glove, and held out his bare hand to Edmund.

"That key's note has been on the Section's list of impossible sounds for two hundred years," he said. "I'm glad to be able to cross it off."

Edmund shook his hand. "It's on my desk," he said. "You can have it. I won't be needing it."

Crane put his glove back on. Then he looked at [Mira](/mˈiɹə/).

"If they all start writing," he said quietly, "every family on earth, I want you to know it'll be on your head."

"I know," said [Mira](/mˈiɹə/). "But it was a very good letter."

And for a second, under the neat grey surface, something in Crane's face moved. She could have sworn it was nearly a smile.
