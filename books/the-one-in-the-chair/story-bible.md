# Story Bible: The Reaper and the Furniture (Novel 1, working title)

_Series context, other premises and audio notes: `books/collection.md`._

## 3. World rules (Novel 1)

- **Judgement.** When someone dies they are judged by the emotional residue they leave behind, not by simple good or bad. They become an object reflecting how they were remembered, not necessarily how they actually were. Examples so far: a dentist remembered as warm and reliable becomes a teapot; a man everyone let wipe their feet on becomes a doormat.
- **Reapers** do not kill. They process the newly dead through judgement, catalogue what they become, and file appeals when a classification seems wrong. They are essentially caseworkers in a grief bureaucracy, which gives built-in dark comedy.
- **Objects are aware.** A chair-soul can still feel warmth, cold, being sat on, being ignored. This is the horror hook: the mother is not gone, she is trapped and waiting.
- **Appeals** require proof that the living misremembered the dead, so the work is part detective story. The Office only accepts consistent testimony, and one fair account does not outweigh many agreeing ones. Primary accounts in the dead person's own hand count as unusually strong evidence.
- **No cost to the reaper.** The user explicitly rejected an idea where each appeal drains something from the reaper. Mira has no ticking clock beyond the case itself.
- **The Office** is the afterlife bureaucracy. Its stationery is grey, stamped and smells of nothing. Time there moves more like weather than a schedule. The city the dead live in exists "slightly sideways" from the living one. The newly dead keep human shape briefly, as a courtesy, before the sentence takes hold. Crossing messages between the living and dead worlds is rare, expensive and unsanctioned, and the Office fears it would collapse the appeals system.

## 4. Cast

- **Mira** is the protagonist, a reaper for about three years. She is sharp-tongued, chronically exhausted and deadpan, and uses gallows humour to cope. She became a reaper specifically to fix her mother's case and has quietly built appeals expertise, bending rules without getting caught. At home she sits on the floor beside the chair rather than in it.
- **Elin**, her mother, ran a women's shelter on almost no money or sleep. She was classified as negligent after a woman in her care left one night and did not return safely. She is now a dark wooden chair with a thinning cushion and arms worn smooth. She communicates through creaks and warmth: the cushion is always warm when Mira rests against it. Mira has spent about three years learning to read these.
- **The supervisor** is unnamed so far (the user chose to leave him mysterious). He is a centuries-old former reaper, now part administrator and part archivist, and thoroughly worn down. His office is on the third floor behind a door with no nameplate. He is not evil. He represents what Mira could become if she stops fighting. He turns out to be a quiet ally: he advises her to file the appeal formally and openly, and asks her to come to him before acting on whatever she learns about the letter's origin.
- **The three judges** on the appeals panel have faces that never quite resolve into anything memorable, which is deliberate. The centre judge is flat and procedural. The left-hand judge is dry and slightly more humane.
- **A grey clerk** reads evidence aloud in the hearing room.

## 5. Chapter summaries (Novel 1)

**Chapter One: Intake.** Mira processes a dentist who is being turned into a teapot. He considers appealing but decides he can live with how he is remembered. This shows the system and Mira's voice. She finishes seventeen more intakes, goes home and sits beside her mother's chair. The chair creaks and the cushion is warm. Mira's mother's file (four digits, sealed, under appeal for nearly four years) carries the notice "Case remains open. Do not resubmit without new evidence." The chapter ends with Mira wondering where she could find any.

**Chapter Two: New Evidence.** A cream-coloured, handwritten, never-sent letter from Elin appears on Mira's desk, smelling of an old drawer in the living world. In it Elin explains she let the woman leave the shelter because she asked for air and Elin would not be "one more person locking a door on her". She accepts what happened afterwards is hers to carry, but says the mistake was a world where trusting the woman was not safe. Mira feels hope for the first time in four years and wonders how the letter reached her, suspecting a price attached.

**Chapter Three: The Supervisor.** Mira takes the letter to the supervisor. He says nobody in the Office retrieves personal letters from the living world, and that whoever sent it either made a reckless kindness or wants something from her. He warns that the Office would hate any precedent of the living reaching the dead. He advises a full formal appeal, citing the letter as primary testimony and openly admitting she does not know where it came from. He asks her to come to him first once she finds out.

**Chapter Four: The Hearing.** Case 4471 goes before three judges in a windowless, bone-coloured room. The letter is read into the record and the panel finds it compelling, saying it reframes the incident as respect for another person's autonomy rather than negligence. But the provenance is unverified and one letter is not enough. The classification is **suspended, not overturned**. If Mira can show where the letter came from and that no boundary between the worlds was violated, the panel will reconvene. At home the chair creaks as if asking "well?", and later holds her more firmly, as if saying "be careful". Mira resolves to find out who sent it, which means finding out who could.

## 6. Open threads and next steps

- **Who sent the letter, and what do they want?** This is the engine of the next chapters. Candidates are not yet decided: someone in the Office, someone in the living world (possibly connected to the woman from the shelter), or another soul.
- **The supervisor's name and backstory** are still open.
- **Mira's surname** is not decided.
- **The woman who left the shelter** has had no further development. Her fate and her own testimony could matter for the appeal.
- **Episodic structure.** The original idea was that each "case" could be a different soul Mira helps through appeals, alongside the main arc about her mother.
- **Possible small continuity point.** Mira has been a reaper for about three years but has been working her mother's case for four, which implies she was already building the case before joining. Decide whether to keep that or adjust.

## 7. Tone and style notes

- Dry, deadpan, darkly funny, with real grief underneath. The comedy and the heartbreak should sit in the same scene.
- Short, readable chapters, written to be heard: short sentences, no tables or footnotes, numbers spelled out, awkward names or terms made easy to pronounce.
- Keep the chair wordless. Her presence comes through creaks, warmth and small physical cues.
- The user liked Chapter One a great deal. Keep that voice.


## 9. Decisions (2026-10-14)

- **Mira is dead.** Reapers are souls who take the job instead of facing judgement. Mira died about a year after Elin and chose to work rather than be judged, so that she could fight her mother's case. This explains the timeline: Elin's case has been open four years, Mira has been a reaper three. Mira died in a road accident (ch10: "A wet road, a delivery van, a Tuesday."), about a year after Elin.
- **The supervisor sent the letter**, using an old reaper's crossing key that the archive recorded as destroyed. His name is **Edmund** (revealed ch10). Centuries ago he abandoned an appeal for someone he loved (his sister) and has avoided his own judgement ever since. At the end he chooses to face it.
- **Inspector Crane** of the Office's Boundary Section is the antagonist: procedural, cold, sincerely afraid of precedent. His rule: contraband taints the appeal of whoever trafficked it, not the testimony itself.
- **Ending:** Elin's classification is overturned and she moves on. The chair becomes just a chair; Mira sits in it for the first time and opens the window.
- **The woman from the shelter** stays offstage in Novel 1.
- Chapters 2–4 are redrafted from the summaries in section 5; the chat versions are not used.
