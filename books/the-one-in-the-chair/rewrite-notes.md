# The One in the Chair: Rewrite Notes

Notes from a planning discussion. The intake scenes are the heart of the book and the warm, quirky tone is the priority. Everything else (the mystery plot, the Office's rules) is scaffolding that should support the intake scenes, not compete with them for space.

## Keep exactly as written (do not change)

- **The recurring intake scenes:** teapot dentist, diary woman, mirror man, nightlight boy, umbrella woman. Do not add new intake characters; instead, weave more of the existing intake rhythm into the plot-heavy stretches.
- **The chair and its warmth** as the emotional throughline.
- **The echo payoff:** the forty-one "agreeing" testimonies are really one newspaper headline repeated.

## Confirmed change: the sentencing logic

Current problem: if everyone becomes an object forever and "release" goes nowhere explained, being sentenced means nothing, and Elin's "punishment" is indistinguishable from what everyone gets.

New logic:

- **Who gets turned into an object at all is arbitrary and never explained.** It is not about being good or bad, and not about how strongly someone was remembered (the doormat man, barely remembered, is also sentenced). Treat it with a shrug in the Office's voice: not our department, that's just how death goes sometimes. Readers should not feel short-changed by this.
- **The real judgement is the length of the term.** Most objects serve a limited, unspecified term, then move on (some may be put back to use, in a good way).
- **Indefinite is the actual punishment**, reserved for cases like Elin's. Mira is fighting to get her mother a term instead of "forever".
- **Hints about terms stay quiet and unspoken.** No explicit numbers ("two years" is too cheap and clock-like). Only the odd light remark, e.g. "it's not forever", "this one won't take long".
- The appeal and hearing structure should not need big changes; it should largely work itself out once classification becomes term length.
- Where release leads does not need explaining.

## New idea to carry forward

- Objects are not just filed into storage; they are **placed somewhere**, set back into the world. Where an object ends up matters. Keep the line **"put me somewhere nice"** (from a rejected draft scene) and use the idea.

## Open problems

### 1. How Mira discovers the origin of the letter is too easy and flat
The forgery sequence (archive, register, drawer, file, Edmund) resolves too quickly. Needs more friction: investigation, false leads, stakes.

### 2. How the letter physically reaches Mira's desk
Currently Edmund alone with a spare key, which is too clean and makes him the only plausible suspect for a reader. Options discussed, none chosen yet:

1. Edmund alone, but it costs him more (several crossings, a visible toll).
2. Edmund retrieves it but hands it to a second person inside the Office who places it on her desk.
3. A chain starting on the living side, with Edmund only completing the journey.

The final chain could be more complicated; the aim is a mystery with more than one plausible suspect and less convenience.

### 3. The ledger clerk
He is suspected by Mira but is too thin as a character. If he carries real suspicion, he needs to be developed into a proper character first.

### 4. Plot chapters have no intakes
During the Boundary Section stretch (roughly chapters 5 to 9), the intake scenes mostly vanish just as the plot gets busy. Look for ways to keep intake scenes present in those chapters using the existing characters.

## Minor continuity wrinkle (separate from the above)

In Chapter Four the panel's condition for reconvening is that no boundary violation is shown. In Chapter Twelve Edmund admits the crossing and the panel rules via Article Nine anyway, without revisiting that condition. May resolve itself in the rewrite, but worth checking.
