# Continuity: The Worst Liar in Pennyford

Append-only facts established in the draft. `chNN | fact`.

| Ch | Fact |
|----|------|
| 01 | Michaelmas Fair is a Saturday. Nell, 19, Market Weigher for two years; sixteen brass weights, the great pound down to the quarter-ounce. |
| 01 | Mrs Gammidge's cheese: four pounds three ounces; Nell says "It's sweaty". At six Nell told the vicar his sermon was long, in church. |
| 01 | Gran claims to be sixty-one; she is seventy-four. Gran's pie stall sells onion pies. |
| 01 | Mr Hobday, landlord of the Half Moon, counts ninety-nine in; eighty-three in the room facing the fire (Nell counts twice). |
| 01 | Telling 1: Josiah Fairweather, great-great-grandfather, built the bridge after the flood two hundred years ago (a cart, a horse and two children drowned at the ford); a penny a crossing. Bridge: five stone arches over the River Penny. |
| 01 | Nell lives across the river from the square (Ladle Lane); the toll house is at the near end of the bridge on her side. Toll keeper: broad, sleepy, red scarf. |
| 02 | Book entry: toll granted to Josiah Fairweather and heirs, 1712 ("seventeen hundred and twelve"). Mrs Thorne lives in the tall house beside the church. |
| 02 | Gran's slip story: aged ten, her aunt in Hollin, over the hills; a pedlar's telling about the miller's short measure; the wife's sister disbelieved it aloud; the miller never got his money back. |
| 02 | Fairweather: "I've been telling stories in four cities, and I've never met one of you." Whistles the Crier's call. |
| 03 | Market day is Monday. Telling 2 (told stall by stall Monday; in force Tuesday dawn): Fairweathers always cried the news; Amos Bell (70) a cobbler since fourteen, Shambles Row, sign "A. Bell, Boots Mended". Amos was Crier thirty-one years, had the bell from Crier Pask. |
| 03 | Crier's blue coat too short in the arms; the green sleeves show. Bell rung three strokes, a pause, three strokes. Moot: three weeks from the fair Saturday (eighteen days from Tuesday). |
| 04 | Nell told Mrs Thorne her lemon cake was dry. The bridge meeting: the following Saturday afternoon, sixty-one people (Mr Hobday counting). No moss on the toll house roof; bright hinges; the worn path runs straight through the gate. |
| 04 | Fairweather's firming story: the great freeze a hundred years ago, gate burned; toll paid into a cup for twenty years; the gate rebuilt by his father; a child fell from the parapet (Mrs Lusk's great-grandmother paid the toll for life). |
| 04 | Telling 3 that night at the Half Moon: "Nell Pardy, the biggest liar in Pennyford since the day she could talk." |
| 05 | Under telling 3: short weight; told the vicar his wife was sweet on the curate; told the Hobday boys their mother ran off with a tinker. Mr Hobday becomes Weigher (Monday). |
| 05 | Telling 4 (Sunday night at the Half Moon): Ladle Lane cottages always Mr L. Fairweather's. Rent letter: Mrs Hester Pardy, tenant, Number Four Ladle Lane, three pounds overdue, by Friday or the Reeve's bailiff. Gran's grandfather's carved name over the back door vanishes. |
| 06 | Fairweather's offer (Monday evening): say "I made the free bridge up" at the Moot (twelve days off); cottage in Gran's name for her lifetime at a peppercorn rent; bailiff held off until the Moot. Planned telling: the founding of Pennyford. |
| 06 | Luke Sallow: Ruth Sallow's boy from the old tan-yard (now nettles); told whoppers (uncle a lord, an angel on the church roof); ran off at twelve from the bridge; Ruth died a year or two later. About thirty-five now. |
| 06 | Gran heard Nell practise the lie; "I'll go with you over my own memory." |
| 07 | The Moot: Nell counts three hundred and four, then three hundred and nine. Fairweather begins: Abel Fairweather came over the hills three hundred years ago. Telling on the cross: "Nell Pardy has been able to lie as easily as breathing since the day she was born." |
| 07 | Nell: "He's Luke Sallow… and he's a thief." Mrs Thorne: lemon cake, "too much flour". The doubt count passes two hundred and twenty; all tellings slip. |
| 08 | Toll house gone; Amos takes back the bell; Book page restored. Luke Sallow banished from sunrise next day, repays tolls (purse to Mr Hobday; Nell counts). Nell fined one penny. |
| 08 | Gran gives him two onion pies. "The drowned children… That part was true." Amos gives Nell the bell; Nell becomes Crier, keeps the weigh-house. |
| 08 | (second edition) Nell sworn in as Crier by Mrs Thorne on the next market day, morning, in the tall house by the church; hand on the Book; Amos and Mr Hobday witnesses. Then cries at noon. |
| 06 | (second edition) The mirror rehearsal is not a lie (rule 10); Nell's fear is that practice makes the lie at the Moot possible. |
