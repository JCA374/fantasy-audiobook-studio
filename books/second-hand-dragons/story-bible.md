# Story Bible: Second-Hand Dragons

_Series context and house style: `books/collection.md`. Ideation, options and parked material: `seed.md`._

## Quick Reference

**Genre:** Cosy winter fantasy, comic and warm, with a real cost underneath. British spelling.
**Logline:** Pawned by a widow and furious about it, a half-blind old dragon tells how the one shop girl it couldn't scare off came to keep the city's last fire alive.
**Comparable Titles:** Diana Wynne Jones's *Howl's Moving Castle* (a grumpy fire creature in a household; keep our dragon clearly different), Terry Pratchett's *Going Postal* (warmth under dryness, a city with guilds), Dickens's *A Christmas Carol* (winter, warmth, what money can't buy).
**Target Length:** about 17k words in 13 chapters of 1,100–1,500 words (author, 2026-10-18).
**Narration:** first person, the dragon, past tense. Narrator voice open (books 1–2 used af_heart).
**Status:** Stage 1, story bible (2026-10-18).

## Premise & Theme

### Core Premise
In the city of Greyhithe, fire only stays warm if it is given. Fire that is sold gives light but no heat, and after
forty years of the Lamplighters' Guild selling light by the hour, the city's given hearths have nearly all gone
out. In late autumn a widow pawns an old, half-blind dragon at Hobb's Pledges and Loans. Every hearth in the city
was first lit from its flame, and that flame is now failing. The only one it lets near it is Tansy, sixteen, who
was left at the shop as a pledge herself eleven years ago. At the Midwinter auction both of them are to be sold.

### Thematic Question
Is anything worth keeping if it's only kept for its use?

### Moral Argument
What you keep for its use you can always sell, and what you sell goes cold. Warmth only passes between people
when it is given, and giving costs the giver something real. Tansy learns she is kept because she is loved, not
because she is useful. Aldevar learns that a fire hoarded dies with you, and a fire given goes on.

### Recurring Motifs
- **The bell over the shop door.** It rings at the start of every chapter. The last chapter ends on the bell ringing for someone coming home.
- **"Can you spare a light?"** Said three times, each meaning something different (proposal): Tansy asking a neighbour (ch3); Tansy asking Aldevar for the street, refused; and at the climax Aldevar asking it of Tansy, for itself.
- **Warm against cold** is the book's texture: grey coals, frost on lamp glass, the counter's cold glass, and the shop's dead stove, which is warm at the end.
- **Tickets and ledgers.** The pawn ticket Aldevar bites, Tansy's ledger entry, the torn ticket and the word *given*.

## Plot Foundation

Chapter numbers are a rough guide for 13 chapters (proposal); `outline.md` (Stage 2) fixes them.

### Three-Act Structure

**Act I: The Pledge** (ch1–3)
- Ordinary world: the cold shop on Tallow Lane in the last weeks of autumn. Mrs Hobb's rule (never get attached to stock), Quill on the shelf, Tansy keeping the ledger, a stove that went out years ago.
- Inciting incident: Mrs Fennick brings Aldevar in a coal scuttle and pawns it. It bites the ticket, snaps at Mrs Hobb and lets nobody near it except Tansy (ch1).
- Protagonist's initial response: Aldevar intends to sulk in the back room until the widow comes back or until it goes out, whichever is first. Tansy treats it as one more pledge to look after.
- Act I climax: the rule is shown (a live coal sold over the counter goes grey in a woman's palm, ch2; Tansy borrows a light next door, ch3), and the Guild hears there is a dragon on Tallow Lane.

**Act II-A: Thursdays** (ch4–6)
- New world: the widow's Thursday visits; Quill's eleventh-anniversary speech; the Guild lamps hissing outside, bright and useless.
- Rising complications: Mr Strand inspects Aldevar with a thermometer and a contract and announces the Guild will bid at Midwinter. Mrs Hobb, who is nearly broke, does not refuse. Tansy asks Aldevar to spare a light for the street, and it refuses.

**Midpoint** (ch7)
- What happens: Tansy finds her own entry in the ledger ("one girl, aged five, pledged against four crowns") and her lot number on the Midwinter list. At sixteen an unredeemed person-pledge must be listed (proposal).
- How it changes everything: she and Aldevar are both lots in the same sale. Her "I'm only kept while I'm useful" now has a date on it, and Aldevar, who has been rude about pledges, is one of them.

**Act II-B: Given** (ch8–10)
- Stakes raised: Aldevar gives its first flame, to Tansy's freezing candle, and visibly dims; the shop is cold for a day (ch8). The cost is now known to everyone.
- Stripping away support: the night walk to Mrs Fennick's cold house (ch9), where Aldevar learns why she pawned it, and that she let her own given hearth go out after her husband died.
- All is lost (ch10): Tansy finds "not for sale" written beside her entry every year in pencil and rubbed out, and reads it as proof that Mrs Hobb always meant to sell her. Mrs Hobb agrees the Guild's reserve price. Aldevar is too weak to leave the back room.

**Act III: Midwinter** (ch11–13)
- Dark night of the soul: Aldevar's. Its fire is nearly out, and the lie ("the fire is mine, and it ends with me") is the only thing it has left.
- Final confrontation: the Midwinter auction (ch11). Mrs Fennick bids and then stops, because anything she buys back will burn cold for her (proposal). The Guild wins. Before the handover, Aldevar asks Tansy if she can spare a light, for itself, and gives her its fire freely.
- Resolution: Tansy carries the fire out of the hall and down Tallow Lane, house to house (ch12), told as Aldevar feels the warmth spread across a city it can't see. The Guild has a dragon it paid for, whose fire burns cold for them; Mr Strand gives it back to the shop for nothing, the only way it could warm anyone (proposal). Mrs Hobb tears up Tansy's ticket and writes *given* in the ledger. Quill leaves the shelf. Aldevar, small and dim, becomes the shop's hearth for a few more winters (ch13).

### Subplots
1. **The widow** (author's choice): Mrs Fennick visits every Thursday and never redeems the dragon. She could afford to, but she can't bear it at home, because it smells of her husband. She told Mrs Hobb it was for the rent. At the auction she has to choose, and she lets it go so that its fire can be given. In ch12 the first house Tansy lights is hers (proposal).

## Character Registry

### Narrator and protagonist: Aldevar ("the Undimmed", it insists)

**Basics**
- Age: centuries; it lit the founders' first hearth when Greyhithe was a ford and some huts (proposal).
- Occupation: retired familiar to Master Fennick, wizard, for sixty years (proposal). Now a pledge.
- Physical description: dragons shrink as their fire fails. It was once the size of a barn; now it is the size of a large cat and fits in a coal scuttle. Scales the colour of old pennies, a milky film over both eyes, a wheeze like a bellows with a hole in it.

**Psychology**
- Want: to be bought back by the widow, or failing that, to go out quietly in the back room.
- Need: to pass its fire on before it dies.
- Wound: it outlived the one person who knew it.
- Lie: "The fire is mine, and it ends with me."
- Arc type: positive.

**Voice Notes**
- Vocabulary: pedantic and vain about its past, formal, proud of long words, out of date about the city ("when I last looked, the Corn Exchange was a field"). Rude to everyone, ruder to the owl.
- Speech patterns: long, well-built sentences that end on a short barb. Corrects people. Never says it's frightened.
- What it notices: smell, warmth, sound, the weight of footsteps. Sight is light, dark and blurred shapes. It knows people by smell (Tansy: ink, soap and the cold; Mrs Hobb: brass polish; the widow: lavender and the wizard's pipe).
- Frame (proposal): it tells the story a few winters later, from the shop hearth. It can tease what's coming, but never gives the ending away.

**Key Relationships**
- Tansy: the only one it lets near, because she is the only one who asks before touching ("May I?").
- Mrs Fennick: grief it won't admit, and a smell it can't bear.
- Quill: a sparring partner, and also a pledge nobody came back for.

---

### Protagonist: Tansy

**Basics**
- Age: sixteen.
- Occupation: shop girl at Hobb's Pledges and Loans; keeps the ledger.
- Physical description: thin, with ink on her fingers and three cardigans on at once. Aldevar describes her by smell and sound: quick light steps, a pencil behind her ear that it hears her chew.

**Psychology**
- Want: to be useful enough that she gets kept.
- Need: to believe she is worth keeping for her own sake.
- Wound: at five she was left at the shop as a pledge for her family's debt of four crowns, and nobody came back.
- Lie: "I'm only kept while I'm useful."
- Arc type: positive.

**Voice Notes**
- Vocabulary: plain, exact, numerate. She talks in prices, dates and rules, and quotes the shop's rules back at people.
- Speech patterns: short questions, and lists. Not plucky: careful, and a little frightening about procedure.
- What she notices: what things are worth, what's missing, who hasn't paid.

**Key Relationships**
- Mrs Hobb: the only family she has, and the person who could sell her.
- Aldevar: another unredeemed pledge, and one that's rude to her in a way that feels like respect.
- Quill: arrived the same year she did.

---

### Opposing force: the Midwinter auction and the Lamplighters' Guild

**The auction (the clock).** By city custom every pledge falls due at Midwinter, and unredeemed pledges are sold at the auction hall (proposal: "the Midwinter Reckoning"). Nobody is to blame for it. It's simply a date.

**Mr Strand, the Guild's agent (minor)**
- Age: forties.
- Occupation: agent of the Lamplighters' Guild, which holds the city's charter to sell fire.
- Physical description: smells of lamp oil and starch. Gloves, a thermometer in a leather case, a contract.
- Goal: to buy the dragon at Midwinter.
- Motivation (proposal): he sincerely believes the Guild's lamps are cold because they are lit from weak, second-hand flame, and that buying the *first* fire will make them warm. He thinks "sold fire burns cold" is a grandmothers' tale.
- Method: polite, legal and well funded. He makes a fair offer to a shop that badly needs the money.
- Why he's a worthy opponent: the law, the money and common sense are on his side, and he is not cruel. He's simply wrong about how warmth works.

### Supporting Cast

**Mrs Hobb**: the pawnbroker (obstacle, then ally)
- Relationship to Tansy: took her as a pledge eleven years ago and raised her without ever saying so.
- Key traits: fifties, sharp about money and very good at her job. Her father's rule was "never get attached to stock". The shop is nearly broke and the stove went out years ago; she buys Guild light.
- Arc (proposal): she has kept Tansy off the auction list for eleven years, writing "not for sale" in pencil and rubbing it out, because freeing her would mean Tansy might leave. She learns that keeping something isn't the same as holding on to it, and she tears up the ticket.

**Mrs Fennick**: the widow (the cost, and a mirror)
- Relationship to Aldevar: her late husband's familiar; she pawned it.
- Key traits: lavender and damp, good gloves gone thin. Brisk, polite and grieving. She talks to the dragon on Thursdays about her husband while it pretends to sleep.
- Arc: see Subplots.

**Quill**: a moulting owl (comic ally)
- Relationship: eleven years on the shelf, a pledge nobody came back for. It arrived the same year as Tansy (proposal).
- Key traits: treats being on the shelf as a career. Makes speeches, keeps count of everything, sheds feathers on the ledger. Aldevar's sparring partner.
- Arc: at the end it leaves the shelf, freely, and stays.

## World Details

### Setting
**Primary Location:** Greyhithe, a cold river city of guilds, ledgers and chimneys that mostly don't smoke.
**Time Period:** a fantasy world without a named era: gas-lamp feel (lamps, ledgers, coal), no railways or machines. Crowns and pennies.
**Story time:** about six weeks, from the first frost to Midwinter, so six Thursdays (proposal).
**Key Locations:**
1. **Hobb's Pledges and Loans** (front counter, the shelf, the back room): brass polish and old wool / the door bell and the ledger's pages / worn, cold counter glass.
2. **Tallow Lane** (the street and the neighbours' doors): coal smoke from the few hearths left / the hiss of Guild lamps / frost underfoot.
3. **Mrs Fennick's house**: lavender and damp / a stopped clock, so silence / a cold iron grate.
4. **The auction hall**: wet coats and candle wax / the auctioneer's patter and the gavel / hard benches.

There's no Guild hall scene: the Guild comes to the shop and to the auction.

### Sensory Palette
- Sights (blurred, through Aldevar's eyes): bright Guild lamps with no warmth in them, a grey coal, the glow of a candle that's warm.
- Sounds: the shop bell, lamps hissing, the ledger's pages, Quill's feathers, Aldevar's bellows wheeze, the gavel.
- Smells: brass polish, wool, ink, lavender, pipe smoke, lamp oil, coal smoke, snow.
- Textures: cold glass, frost, scales like old pennies, warm brick at the end.
- Tastes: weak tea, the soot in the air.

### Social/Political Context
- The Lamplighters' Guild got the charter to sell fire forty years ago (proposal). Bought light was cheap and convenient, so people let their given hearths go out, and every winter there are fewer warm houses. The Guild blames hard winters.
- Pawn law treats familiars as property, so they can be pledged and sold. Persons can be pledged into service for a debt, an old law nobody likes to talk about (rules 15–16).
- "Borrowing a light" from a neighbour is a social bond. Refusing one is the worst insult in Greyhithe.

### Rules of the World (the author's logic; revised 2026-10-20)

_The reader learns these one at a time (see the reveal ladder below). Nobody in the book ever states them all; this list is for the author, so that every scene obeys the same rules._

**Fire**
1. **Only a dragon makes fire from nothing.** Everyone else can only pass a flame on. Every flame in Greyhithe comes from the Founders' Fire, which Aldevar lit.
2. **A flame is warm when it is given:** passed on freely by the person it belongs to, with nothing taken for it. Asking ("Can you spare a light?") is the custom, but it isn't required (ch8 is unasked).
3. **Payment cools it.** Paying for the flame, even a penny, makes it cold: it gives light but no heat. A flame taken without leave (stolen) is cold too. Coal, wax and oil can be bought; only the flame counts.
4. **A paid-for flame is cold for good, and so is every flame lit from it** ("cold catches"). Giving a cold flame on doesn't warm it: Mr Abel could not have warmed his coal by handing it to his wife. The only cure is to put the cold flame out and light the wick again from a given one (ch12: Tansy pinches out the woman's Guild flame first). What counts is where the flame now burning came from.
5. **A dragon is not a flame.** Every flame a dragon makes is new, and it belongs to whoever owns the dragon. If the owner paid for the dragon, its new flames are bought, and cold for that owner and anyone they sell to. If a bought dragon is given away for nothing, the new owner has paid nothing, and its new flames are warm again (Strand's gift, ch12). This never applies to a single flame; see rule 4. Mrs Fennick (ch11) could in principle buy him and then give him away, but she says she couldn't give him away twice.
6. **You can only give what is yours.** A dragon's fire is its own until somebody pays for it. Pledging, holding and forfeit are not paying, and nobody has ever bought Aldevar (ch11), so until noon on Midwinter Day his fire is his to give. At noon the Guild's payment is entered, "all fire arising from it" becomes the Guild's, and after that Aldevar has nothing he can give.
7. **The given network is thinning.** A given hearth that goes out can only be lit again from someone else's gift. People who can't or won't ask put their coal on a Guild flame: it smokes, it doesn't warm, and the Guild says it's the weather. So warm hearths are islands, and every one that goes out is gone unless a neighbour relights it. What stops the spread is not coal but asking: pride, habit, and not wanting to admit you have paid for cold for years.

**Dragons**
8. **A dragon is the size of its fire.** The fire burns down over the centuries, as any fire does.
9. **A dragon can't live on its own fire.** It is fed by a given hearth that somebody else keeps beside it. The hearth doesn't refill the dragon; it only stops the leak. For forty years (since their wedding) that was the Fennicks' grate, which Aldevar lit as a wedding present and which they kept for him. It went out the week the wizard died, and that is when Aldevar began to go out. A given candle helps a little (ch3). A cold shop is starvation.
10. **Giving costs the dragon.** Every flame he gives takes a piece of his fire outright (author's cost "a"). Ordinary people who pass a light on lose nothing. The payoff (ch13): his own flame comes back to him as a gift, through Tansy's candle into the stove, which is the only way a dragon can be warmed. This answers his lie, "the fire is mine, and it ends with me".

**How society treats it:** everybody's grandmother says sold fire burns cold. The Guild calls that superstition, says lamps are for light, and blames the cold on hard winters. Strand sincerely believes the Guild's flame is "a copy of a copy" worn thin.

### Pledges and the Reckoning

11. **A pledge is a loan with a thing held against it.** The pledger still owns the thing and can redeem it at any time before Midwinter by repaying the loan and the interest. **Redeeming is not buying**: the thing comes home never sold, and a redeemed dragon's fire stays warm.
12. **At Midwinter every unredeemed pledge is forfeit to the lender.** The lender must sell it at the public Reckoning, so that owners get one last chance to buy their things back. **The lender keeps the price.** After Midwinter the old owner can only *buy*, and bought fire is cold for her (ch11).
13. **A reserve is the seller's floor.** The Guild asks Mrs Hobb to set Aldevar's reserve at two hundred crowns, and promises to bid it. The point is that nobody can buy him back cheaply, and that means Mrs Fennick, while the shop is guaranteed two hundred crowns (ch5). Signing it is Mrs Hobb's choice, and her temptation.
14. **A Reckoning sale is entered in the magistrates' book at noon on Midwinter Day,** and ownership passes then. Until noon a lot still belongs to its old owner.
15. **Person-pledges** (an old law nobody likes): a person pledged as a child is listed when she comes of age at sixteen. The buyer gets her service until she is one-and-twenty.
16. **A lender can forgive a debt.** No debt means no pledge, and no pledge means nothing to list. This is the only way to withdraw a lot. Mrs Hobb could have done it any time in eleven years and didn't, for fear that Tansy, freed, would leave.
17. **Quill was never a pledge.** The Great Ambrose paid for his keep and never borrowed a penny against him, so there is no debt to fall due. Quill is a lodger who believes he is stock.
