# Outline: Second-Hand Dragons

**Target:** about 17,000 words in 13 chapters (about 100 minutes of audio).
**POV:** one, Aldevar, first person, past tense. It tells the story a few winters later from the shop hearth, and only
narrates what it hears, smells and feels; scenes away from it come through walls, windows and voices.
**Status:** draft for author approval, 2026-10-18. Built from `story-bible.md` (all proposals accepted) and `seed.md`.

## Throughlines

- **Aldevar's lie:** "The fire is mine, and it ends with me." **Truth:** a fire hoarded dies with you; a fire given goes on.
- **Tansy's lie:** "I'm only kept while I'm useful." **Truth:** she is kept because she is loved.
- **The clock:** the Midwinter Reckoning, six Thursdays away in ch1.
- **The bell:** every chapter opens with the shop bell (who comes in, or that nobody does). Ch13 ends on it ringing for someone coming home.
- **"Can you spare a light?"** ch3 (Tansy to a neighbour, given); ch5 (Tansy to Aldevar, for the street, refused); ch11 (Aldevar to Tansy, for itself).
- **The rule** is shown before the climax uses it: sold fire goes cold (ch2), given fire stays warm (ch3), giving costs Aldevar (ch8), bought creature means bought fire (ch11, set up in ch5 by Strand's contract).

## Chapters

### Act I: The Pledge

**1. Pledged** (1,300 words, 0–8%). First Thursday, first frost.
- Hook, first line: Aldevar was pawned on a Thursday, in a coal scuttle, for eleven crowns, and would like that on the record.
- The shop by smell and sound: brass polish, wool, the cold counter glass, Quill shuffling on the shelf, Tansy's pencil.
- Mrs Fennick sets the scuttle on the counter: "It's for the rent." Aldevar notes she is lying, by smell (set piece 1). It bites the ticket.
- It snaps at Mrs Hobb. Tansy asks "May I?" before lifting it, and it lets her: the only one who asks (Q Factor plant).
- Mrs Hobb's rule out loud: never get attached to stock. The ticket's terms: redeemable until Midwinter, then the Reckoning.
- Night in the back room beside the dead stove. Aldevar intends to sulk until the widow comes back or it goes out, whichever is first.
- Beats: Hook, Inciting Event, Care Package (Tansy and Quill: she warms his water in her hands), Argument Against Transformation (Aldevar: "Fire is not a thing one lends").
- Closing hook: through the wall, Tansy counting the days to Midwinter in the ledger, out loud, as she does with every pledge.

**2. Cold Coal** (1,250 words, 8–15%)
- Bell: a woman with a live coal in a tin, wanting to pawn it. Mrs Hobb refuses: fire doesn't keep on a shelf.
- In the queue a man offers her a penny for it. The coin changes hands and the coal goes grey and cold in his palm (set piece 2). Aldevar, behind the curtain, hears the hiss stop and smells the warmth leave.
- Tansy, matter-of-fact, explains it to the dragon as a price list: given fire, warm; sold fire, light only. Aldevar already knows, and is insulted to be told.
- Dusk: the Guild lamplighters' round on Tallow Lane (set piece 4). Hiss, frost on lamp glass, bright and nobody warm. Two shillings an hour for a lamp in a room.
- Trouble Brewing: a lamplighter stops at the window and sniffs. Dragons smell of hot pennies.
- Closing hook: Aldevar's own fire is lower in the morning than the night before. It doesn't mention this.

**3. A Light Next Door** (1,300 words, 15–23%). Second Thursday.
- Bell: nobody. Mrs Fennick doesn't come.
- Tansy's room is so cold her ink freezes. She knocks next door: "Can you spare a light?" (first time; set piece 3). Aldevar hears the ritual through the wall, the neighbour's "Gladly", and the warmth she carries back down the stairs.
- She puts the given candle in the back room, with Quill and the dragon, not in her own room. Aldevar notices.
- By its warmth, Aldevar boasts: it lit the founders' first hearth when Greyhithe was a ford and some huts. Every warm fire in the city is a cousin of its own.
- Key Event: Tansy realises what is in her coal scuttle, and goes very quiet.
- Closing hook: a card under the door in the morning, smelling of lamp oil and starch: *The Lamplighters' Guild presents its compliments.*

**4. Thursdays** (1,300 words, 23–31%). Third Thursday.
- Bell: Mrs Fennick, lavender and damp. She sits by the scuttle and talks about her husband's pipe, his bad jokes, the way he hummed (set piece 5). Aldevar pretends to sleep and hears every word.
- Tansy, at the door, asks her straight out: will she redeem him? "Not yet."
- Mrs Hobb reads the Guild's letter: intent to bid at the Reckoning. She doesn't put it in the stove. She puts it in the drawer.
- First Plot Point / Doorway #1: Aldevar could call after the widow as she leaves. It has a voice like a cathedral organ when it wants one. It doesn't, out of pride, and from now on it's going to the Reckoning.
- Closing hook: Mrs Hobb, alone, writing in the ledger; the scrape of a rubber.

### Act II-A: Thursdays

**5. The Thermometer** (1,300 words, 31–38%). Week four.
- Bell: Mr Strand, gloves, starch, a thermometer in a leather case, a contract (set piece 9).
- He measures the dragon's heat, politely, and explains the Guild's theory: their lamps are cold because they're lit from weak, second-hand flame; the *first* fire will warm them. He thinks "sold fire burns cold" is a grandmothers' tale.
- Contract terms read aloud: the creature and "all fire arising from it" become Guild property (plants the bought-creature rule).
- Kick in the Shins / First Pinch Point: the Guild's reserve, two hundred crowns. Mrs Hobb, who owes the coal merchant and the landlord, doesn't refuse. Strand, leaving: "Every unredeemed pledge is listed. Of every kind."
- After he's gone, Tansy kneels by the scuttle. "Then give it away first, before they own it. Can you spare a light?" (second time). Aldevar says no. It's his fire; it's nearly all he has left.
- Closing hook: Tansy doesn't argue. That's worse.

**6. Eleven Years** (1,200 words, 38–45%)
- Bell: the coal merchant, wanting paying. Mrs Hobb pays in pledges.
- Quill marks eleven years on the shelf with a speech (set piece 6): dignity, endurance, the moult. "The girl and I are of the same year." Comedy, with Aldevar heckling.
- Pet the Dog: Tansy gives Quill her own hot brick for the night, and goes cold. Aldevar, who could warm them both, doesn't, and doesn't sleep.
- Tansy goes to the old ledgers to check Quill's date for his speech.
- Closing hook: the turning of old pages stops. A long silence, then Tansy reading one line under her breath.

**7. Lot Nineteen** (1,350 words, 45–53%). Fourth Thursday.
- Midpoint / Moment of Truth: the line was hers: "one girl, aged five, pledged against four crowns" (set piece 7). In the drawer, the Reckoning list in Mrs Hobb's hand: Lot nineteen. At sixteen, by law, a person-pledge must be listed.
- Mrs Hobb, asked, says only: "It's the law at sixteen." Nothing else. Tansy says "Of course," and does the accounts.
- Mirror Moment, both of them: Tansy, by the scuttle that night: "What am I, then? Stock." Aldevar, a pledge who has been rude about pledges all book, finds it doesn't have the words, and says something kind very badly.
- Mrs Fennick's Thursday visit is short; she smells of having cried.
- The shift: Tansy stops waiting. If she is going to be sold, the street will be warm first. She starts a list.
- Closing hook: Tansy's candle, the given one from ch3, is out, and her hands are blue.

### Act II-B: Given

**8. The First Flame** (1,300 words, 53–61%)
- Bell: the first snow, and nobody.
- Tansy doesn't ask. She's stopped asking. Aldevar gives her candle a flame anyway, unasked (set piece 8): the first thing it has given since the wizard died.
- The cost, seen and heard: it dims, shrinks inside its scales, its wheeze gets worse; the back room is cold for a day. Mrs Hobb sees it and says nothing, which for her is a great deal.
- Second Pinch Point: the cost is now concrete for everyone. One flame took a week of its life, maybe more.
- Tansy, furious with gratitude: "Don't you ever do that again." Aldevar: "I shall do as I like. I am a dragon."
- Closing hook: it asks to be taken to see the widow. It has never once asked anyone for anything.

**9. The Widow's Grate** (1,350 words, 61–69%). Fifth Thursday.
- Night walk: Tansy carries the scuttle through the snow to Mrs Fennick's house (set piece 10), Aldevar narrating by sound and smell: frost, coal smoke, the hiss of each Guild lamp they pass.
- The house: lavender and damp, a stopped clock, so silence; a cold iron grate. Her given hearth, lit from Aldevar years ago, went out the week her husband died, and she let it.
- Subplot turn: "It wasn't for the rent." She pawned it because the dragon smells of his pipe and she couldn't bear it in the house.
- Aldevar offers to light her grate. She refuses: "Not if it costs you." The two of them grieving the same man, badly, in a cold room.
- Closing hook: walking home, Tansy says the Guild's two hundred crowns would save the shop. Aldevar says, "Then I suppose I'm useful," and Tansy stops in the snow.

**10. Pencil** (1,300 words, 69–76%). A week before Midwinter.
- Bell: Mr Strand, to settle the reserve. Mrs Hobb agrees it.
- Tansy reads the ledger in the back room by the dragon's glow, the only light in the shop, and reads her own entry aloud. Beside it, faint, the same pencil words every year, written and rubbed out (set piece 11). She can make out "for sale". She can't make out the "not".
- Third Plot Point / Doorway #2: Tansy's lie looks proven. She was always for sale; it was only a question of when.
- Aldevar faces its own lie: it is too weak to leave the back room. If it does nothing, its fire ends with it in a Guild cage, warm for nobody.
- Lights Out: the shop's coldest night. Tansy packs her one bag for the Reckoning.
- Closing hook: Aldevar, in the dark, makes up its mind, and tells us it won't tell us yet.

### Act III: Midwinter

**11. The Reckoning** (1,500 words, 76–85%). Midwinter.
- Bell: the shop locked behind them.
- The auction hall (set piece 12): wet coats, candle wax, hard benches, the auctioneer's patter. Lots read aloud. Tansy's lot nineteen is on the list.
- Mounting Forces: the Guild's bench, a row of lamplighters, Strand with a purse.
- Aldevar's lot. Mrs Fennick bids against the Guild, and then stops, because anything she buys back will burn cold for her. She'd rather it was given.
- Third Act Turning Point: the gavel. Sold to the Lamplighters' Guild, two hundred and ten crowns.
- Before the handover, Aldevar asks Tansy (third time): "Can you spare a light?" It means: will you take mine. She paid nothing. It gives her its fire, freely, all it can spare.
- Closing hook: the hall smells, for the first time in forty years, of warm candle wax.

**12. House to House** (1,400 words, 85–94%)
- Climactic Moment / Final Battle: Tansy runs the fire out of the hall and down Tallow Lane, house to house (set piece 13). The first house is Mrs Fennick's. Each one lights the next: neighbours knocking on neighbours, "Can you spare a light?" in every voice.
- Told by Aldevar from the hall: it can't see, but it feels the warmth come back into the city street by street, like hearing a choir fill up.
- The Guild's own lamps, lit from a bought dragon, stay cold. Strand holds his thermometer to Aldevar and watches it not rise.
- Strand understands the rule at last, and gives the dragon back to Mrs Hobb for nothing. It is the only way it could warm anyone. His gloves come off to do it.
- Transformation: the dragon gave and is still alive, small and dim; Tansy gave the fire away instead of keeping it.
- Closing hook: Mrs Hobb carries the scuttle home herself, and Tansy hasn't come back yet.

**13. Given** (1,100 words, 94–100%). The days after Midwinter.
- Resolution. The shop stove is lit, warm for the first time in years, with Aldevar curled inside it.
- Mrs Hobb takes Tansy's pledge ticket from the strongbox and tears it up (set piece 14). In the ledger beside the entry she writes, in ink this time: *given*. What the pencil said, all along: *not for sale*.
- Quill leaves the shelf (set piece 15). It flies once round the shop and lands on the stove pipe. It stays, freely.
- Mrs Fennick comes on Thursday, and sits by the stove.
- Frame: Aldevar, a few winters on, still dim, still rude, telling us it is quite comfortable.
- Last line: the bell ringing for someone coming home.

## Structure profile

- Hook: ch1, first line (pawned on a Thursday, in a coal scuttle).
- Inciting Event (~12%): ch1, the pawning; lands early, as the hub wants for a novella.
- Key Event (end of Act 1): ch3, Tansy learns what Aldevar is.
- First Plot Point (25%): ch4. Doorway of No Return #1 content check: Aldevar could call the widow back and chooses not to; from here it is going to the Reckoning.
- First Pinch Point (37.5%): ch5, Strand's inspection, the contract and the reserve.
- Midpoint / Moment of Truth (50%): ch7, Lot nineteen. Mirror Moment content check: "What am I, then? Stock." Both leads face who they are.
- Second Pinch Point (62.5%): ch8 into ch9, the first flame's visible cost.
- Third Plot Point (70–75%): ch10, the pencil. Doorway of No Return #2 content check: Tansy's lie looks true; Aldevar can't leave the back room; the Reckoning is now unavoidable.
- Third Act Turning Point (88%): end of ch11, the gavel.
- Climactic Moment (90–98%): ch12, the fire house to house. Final Battle / Transformation content check: resolves the death stakes set in ch1 (the dragon's fire, the city's cold) and both lies.
- Resolution: ch13, *given*, Quill, the bell.
- Optional signposts planned: Care Package (ch1), Argument Against Transformation (ch1), Trouble Brewing (ch2), Kick in the Shins (ch5), Pet the Dog (ch6), Mounting Forces (ch11), Lights Out (ch10), Q Factor ("May I?" in ch1, paid off as "Can you spare a light?" in ch11).
- Last structure check: Map, 2026-10-18.
