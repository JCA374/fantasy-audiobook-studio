# Continuity: Second-Hand Dragons

Append-only facts established in the draft. `chNN | fact`.

| Ch | Fact |
|----|------|
| 01 | Aldevar pawned on a Thursday (first frost), in a coal scuttle, for eleven crowns. It bit the ticket. |
| 01 | Master Fennick died a fortnight before ch1; carried out of the house on **Ferrier Street**. Aldevar has said nothing since, until ch1. |
| 01 | Aldevar's eyes have been failing for thirty years: light and dark only. It knows people by smell. |
| 01 | Dragons are the size of their fire; once barn-sized, now "a large and disappointed cat". Scales the colour of old pennies; bellows-with-a-hole breathing. Teeth still very good. |
| 01 | "The noise": the sound a chimney makes just before it catches. |
| 01 | Smells: Mrs Fennick = lavender, damp, faint pipe (lavender goes sharp when she lies); Mrs Hobb = brass polish; Tansy = ink, soap, being cold. Tansy chews her pencil, moves with small quick steps. |
| 01 | The shop bell is thin and cracked. Counter is cold glass. Back room through a moth-smelling curtain; the stove there has been cold for years (ash hard as pavement). |
| 01 | Mrs Hobb calls Aldevar "it"; Mrs Fennick calls it "he". Aldevar refers to itself as I. |
| 01 | Pledge terms: redeemable before Midwinter for eleven crowns plus interest; then the Reckoning. Forty-two days to Midwinter on day one (Tansy counts, then recounts: her own date). |
| 01 | Quill: owl, eleven years on the shelf; Tansy warms his water in her hands. |
| 01 | Tansy asked "May I?" before lifting the scuttle: the first person ever to ask. Once carried by a bishop. |
| 01 | Aldevar's first words in a fortnight: "Fire is not a thing one lends." Tansy: "everything in it is for something"; "You're stock now. So am I, more or less." |
| 02 | By day Aldevar's scuttle sits on the front shelf behind the counter, under the window, between a carriage clock and a box of spoons (Mrs Hobb: a pledge that is looked at gets redeemed). |
| 02 | Woman tried to pledge her mother's hearth-coal in a tin; Mrs Hobb refused ("fire doesn't keep on a shelf"). **Mr Abel** (smells of horses, cold room, "colder wife") paid a penny; the coal went cold, then grey, in his palm. Mrs Hobb lent threepence on the tin (a kind lie: "good tin"). |
| 02 | A live given coal makes a tiny breathing tick; it stops when sold. |
| 02 | Lamplighters' Guild: charter to sell fire for forty years; lamps hiss "like a cat asked to leave"; a lamp in your room is two shillings an hour. Mrs Hobb has one Guild lamp at the counter, for reading hallmarks. |
| 02 | Dragons smell of hot pennies. A lamplighter sniffed at the shop window at dusk; Tansy wrote something down. |
| 02 | Aldevar's fire is a little lower each night (about a candle's evening), unused; it tells no one. |
| 02 | Aldevar claims to be older than the city; "I was there when it started." Tansy doesn't quite believe it yet. |
| 03 | second Thursday: bell rang nine times, no Mrs Fennick. Mrs Hobb: "Thursdays are always slow." |
| 03 | At night Aldevar sleeps in the back room; Tansy carries it every night and always asks first. |
| 03 | Tansy's room is over the shop; the stair creaks on the fourth and ninth steps. Her ink froze (last time: when she was nine, says Quill). |
| 03 | Next door on the left: a cobbler (old man, unnamed); his back room shares a wall with the back room. |
| 03 | The ritual: "Can you spare a light?" / "Gladly." / "Thank you kindly." / "Pass it on, love." (First of three "Can you spare a light?") |
| 03 | Tansy put the given candle between Quill's shelf and the scuttle: "Now it's nobody's, so it's everybody's." |
| 03 | The Founders' Fire: a ford, a few huts, a dragon on the hill; it lit their first hearth for nothing because they asked, in a hard winter; nine hundred years ago. Every child knows it as a story. Aldevar: "I was in it." |
| 03 | Tansy has noticed Aldevar is lighter each night ("about as much as a candle burns down"); "I notice what things weigh." |
| 03 | Next morning a card on the mat, lamp oil and starch: "The Lamplighters' Guild presents its compliments." |
| 04 | Third Thursday (day 15; twenty-eight days left): Mrs Fennick visits, sits on Tansy's crate, talks about her husband. |
| 04 | Master Fennick: cherrywood pipe with a chipped bowl, smoked forty years, never cleaned; the house on Ferrier Street smells of it ("like living inside his coat"). Bad jokes told twice. Hummed the same three bars of a song he never finished. Put his feet on Aldevar's back to warm them. |
| 04 | Mrs Fennick once heard Aldevar answer her husband through the study door. |
| 04 | Tansy starts calling Aldevar "him" (Mrs Hobb: it; Mrs Fennick: he). |
| 04 | Tansy: "Will you redeem him?" Mrs Fennick: "Not yet." Tansy echoes Mrs Hobb: "Everybody says that." |
| 04 | Aldevar's organ voice: once stopped a battle on the river, once told a king what it thought of him. It chose not to call her back (pride; can't bear the pipe-smelling house and his empty chair). |
| 04 | Noon: a Guild boy brings a letter. The Guild will bid at the Midwinter Reckoning and will send an agent to inspect. Mrs Hobb put it in the drawer under the counter, with the strongbox. |
| 04 | That night Mrs Hobb turned far back in the ledger, wrote something short, and rubbed it out. |
| 05 | Monday after the third Thursday (day 19): **Edwin Strand** of the Lamplighters' Guild. Wipes both boots; very good leather gloves, folded one inside the other; lamp oil and starch. Says "May I" but means "where is it". |
| 05 | Strand lifted Aldevar's wing bare-handed without asking and put a thermometer under it; didn't flinch at the noise. Reading: "about the same as a cat. A tired cat." Tansy: "You might have asked him." |
| 05 | Strand's theory: lamps are cold because the flame is "a copy of a copy", worn thin; light from the source = warm lamps. Sincere. |
| 05 | Guild reserve: two hundred crowns. Clause: "The creature, and all fire arising from it, shall become the sole property of the Lamplighters' Guild." Envelope kept in the drawer with the letter. |
| 05 | Mrs Hobb paid the coal merchant on Friday with a silver christening cup from the window; the landlord knocked on Saturday. Two hundred crowns would pay both for three years. She neither refused nor agreed. |
| 05 | Strand: the Reckoning list is posted on the first of the month; magistrates "particular this year"; "Every unredeemed pledge is to be listed. Of every kind." |
| 05 | Second "Can you spare a light?": Tansy, kneeling, asks Aldevar to light Tallow Lane before Midwinter. Aldevar: "No." She doesn't argue; does the accounts. |
| 06 | Wednesday: Mrs Hobb paid the coal merchant in pledges (brass candlesticks, a clock that doesn't go, a fur tippet); got two sacks of coal with nothing warm to light it. |
| 06 | Quill was pledged eleven years ago "in the year of the great flood" by a stage magician, **the Great Ambrose**, who said he'd return on Tuesday. Three moults, two roof leaks, "the incident with the ferret"; a suit of armour redeemed by its owner's nephew. "I call it a career." |
| 06 | Quill: "The girl and I are of the same year." Small Tansy sat under the counter writing letters on the backs of old tickets. First time Aldevar hears Tansy laugh. |
| 06 | Tansy's bed brick: warmed nightly at the cobbler's hearth for nothing. She gave it to Quill ("You've only got the one owl"); she has three cardigans. Aldevar could have warmed it and didn't; didn't sleep. |
| 06 | Old ledgers are kept on shelves at the far end of the shop. After midnight Tansy found Quill's entry, then read "One girl. Aged five." |
| 07 | Fourth Thursday (day 22). The ledger line, in an old thick brown-ink hand (Mrs Hobb's father ran the shop then): "One girl, aged five. Pledged against four crowns, by her mother, who made her mark. Redeemable at any time. Interest waived." Between silver sugar tongs and a mantel clock. No names, the mother's mark an X. |
| 07 | Tansy has a key to the counter drawer (keeps the accounts). Reckoning list in Mrs Hobb's hand: "Lot nineteen. One girl, aged sixteen, pledged eleven years ago against four crowns, unredeemed." Due on the first; magistrates want it early. |
| 07 | Mrs Hobb: "It's the law at sixteen. An unredeemed pledge of a person must be listed when the person comes of age. I've no say in it." Tansy: "Of course." |
| 07 | Mrs Fennick visited briefly at eleven, smelling of salt (had cried). |
| 07 | First night Tansy carried Aldevar without asking. "What am I, then? Stock." Aldevar: "you were very badly priced… I should have asked for nine at the very least. Mrs Hobb's father was a fool." Once worshipped by a village for a fortnight. Tansy laughed, then cried. |
| 07 | Tansy's list: every house on Tallow Lane, given hearth or cold. Cobbler lit (until now), Mr Abel not, the tin woman not. "If one warm hearth can light two more…" |
| 07 | Tansy had kept her candle lit nightly from the cobbler's hearth. The cobbler ran out of coal on Tuesday; his fire is out. Her hands blue. |
| 08 | Same night as ch7, a little after midnight (Friday, day 23): Aldevar, unasked, breathes a flame onto Tansy's dead candle. First flame given since the founders' hearth ("not since a hill above a ford and eleven huts"). |
| 08 | Cost: a part of its fire went out; it got smaller ("a week's worth, perhaps more"); greyer at the edges; breath "a bellows with two holes"; can't hold its head up long. |
| 08 | "It is mine. That is rather the point. You can't give away a thing that isn't." |
| 08 | Tansy filled an iron scuttle from Mrs Hobb's coal cellar and relit the cobbler's hearth from the candle. Cobbler: "Pass it on, love." Tansy: "I will. I promise I will." |
| 08 | The back room is colder now: Aldevar had been quietly warming it. Quill's water iced over. Mrs Hobb saw, said nothing, counted the coal (one sack and a half left) and didn't write it down. |
| 08 | First snow on Greyhithe (Friday morning). |
| 08 | "Don't you ever do that again… I'm not worth a week of you." / "I shall do as I like. I am a dragon." |
| 08 | Aldevar asks for something for the first time in nine hundred years: to be taken to see Mrs Fennick, tonight. |
| 09 | Friday night (day 23), nine o'clock: Tansy carries Aldevar to Ferrier Street, the scuttle wrapped in her third cardigan. Mrs Hobb: "Bring him back before morning" (her first "him"). Pledges must never leave the shop (a rule). |
| 09 | Route: Tallow Lane, nineteen Guild lamps to the river (Tansy counts), the **Corn Bridge**, wider streets beyond. Given hearths' smoke smells of supper; bought ones of pennies. Snow squeaks "like a mouse being stepped on". |
| 09 | Ferrier Street: gutter at the corner, a closed baker's, number seven (the Fennicks'), whose third step rocks. Aldevar lived there sixty years. |
| 09 | The house: church-cold; lavender, damp, his pipe in everything; the tall hall clock has stopped, nobody wound it. Mrs Fennick uses a Guild candle. |
| 09 | Aldevar lit the Fennicks' grate forty years ago on their wedding night as a wedding present ("very rude about it"). It went out the week he died; she let it. |
| 09 | "It wasn't for the rent." She pawned Aldevar because it smells of his pipe. Neither of them ever asked what came after the three bars. |
| 09 | Aldevar offered to light the grate; she refused: "Not if it costs you… Not on me." |
| 09 | Tansy: the Guild's two hundred crowns would pay the landlord, coal merchant and roof man. Aldevar: "Then I suppose I'm useful." Tansy stopped dead in the snow. |
| 10 | The week after Ferrier Street: Tansy passed the cobbler's light to the house across the lane, then two more: three warm houses on Tallow Lane, twelve still cold. Mrs Hobb's coal nearly gone. |
| 10 | Aldevar weakening faster since the gift; weighs "about the same as a loaf"; can barely lift its head. Dragons glow faintly when their fire is low (fire showing through worn scales; not a warm light). |
| 10 | The Guild took back its counter lamp on the Monday (Mrs Hobb couldn't pay). |
| 10 | Sixth Thursday (day 36, a week before Midwinter): Strand's reserve signed. "If no one bids higher, the creature is ours at noon on Midwinter Day." Mrs Hobb: "You don't know anything about it, Mr Strand." She stood a long time in the cellar. |
| 10 | Ledger margin: the same few pencil words in Mrs Hobb's hand, written and rubbed out about eleven times; Tansy reads "for sale"; the short first word is rubbed through to a hole. She reads it as "for sale every year". |
| 10 | Tansy first calls Aldevar "Al". |
| 10 | Aldevar faces the lie: in a Guild cage its fire will end with it and warm nobody. |
| 10 | Coldest night in Greyhithe for forty years: river frozen at the edges to the Corn Bridge; window frost met in the middle; Quill's water froze solid. Tansy packed her bag (two cardigans, stockings, a hairbrush). Aldevar made up its mind, unasked. |
| 11 | Midwinter morning: Mrs Hobb pushes a handcart of unredeemed pledges (boots, wedding ring, carriage clock, Aldevar). Tansy carries her bag with the old given candle in it. Quill stays: never listed, because the Great Ambrose paid three years' keep in advance and vanished. Quill: "Bring her back." |
| 11 | Auction hall: far side of the Corn Bridge; hundreds of Guild candles in iron rings; hard benches. The auctioneer's voice "like a cart going over cobbles". |
| 11 | Guild bench at the front: eight or nine lamplighters, poles under the bench; Strand with a full purse. Lot nineteen chalked on the board by the door. |
| 11 | Lot six: wedding ring, bought back for nine shillings. Lot ten: carriage clock, unsold. **Lot twelve: Aldevar**, reserve two hundred crowns. |
| 11 | Bids: Strand 200; Mrs Fennick 205 (she sold Master Fennick's library yesterday); Strand 210. She stops: "If I buy you, you're bought… I'd rather you were given. To someone." |
| 11 | Sold to the Lamplighters' Guild for two hundred and ten crowns. Possession at noon, nine minutes after the gavel. Aldevar was once stolen briefly by a baron; never sold before. |
| 11 | Third "Can you spare a light?": Aldevar to Tansy, meaning "will you take mine". It breathed everything it could spare, and more, into her old candle: "It's yours… It's given." The hall smells of warm wax for the first time in forty years. |
| 12 | After the gift Aldevar would fit in a teacup; grey all over; can't lift its head. |
| 12 | In the hall: Tansy relights a woman's bought candle (she'd been outbid on a shawl); it turns warm; it spreads bench to bench ("like spilt tea across a cloth"). People carry bought candles in winter as habit. The child with the cold stops coughing. |
| 12 | Tansy runs over the Corn Bridge; first house is number seven Ferrier Street; Mrs Fennick went with her; the grate lit. Then all twelve cold houses on Tallow Lane; the tin woman put a given coal back in her tin. Nobody paid, so all of it was warm. |
| 12 | Noon: Strand's thermometer under Aldevar's wing doesn't move. A lamplighter lit his pole from Aldevar's scales: white, bright, no heat. Strand: "It was never the flame. It was the paying… telling them it was the weather." He let his gloves fall unfolded. |
| 12 | Strand gives Aldevar back to Mrs Hobb for nothing. Mrs Hobb keeps the two hundred and ten crowns ("It would only go cold"). |
| 12 | Lot nineteen called; Tansy absent (her bag left on the bench). Mrs Hobb: "Withdrawn… By me. Never you mind how." She carried the scuttle home herself (first time), without asking. Every chimney on Tallow Lane smells of supper. Tansy not back yet. |
| 13 | Midwinter night: Tansy returns late with the flame still burning (a given flame doesn't shrink for being shared); fire spreading past the walls. Mrs Hobb waited six hours in her coat on the crate. "He's your dragon." |
| 13 | Tansy relit the back-room stove (last half sack of coal) from her candle; set Aldevar inside on the warm coal, asking first; Aldevar said yes aloud. |
| 13 | Next morning: Mrs Hobb tore up Tansy's pledge ticket (her half; the mother's half never returned): "You can't buy back what was never mine to sell." Wrote "Given" in ink in the ledger margin. The pencil said "not for sale", every year since Tansy's sixth birthday. |
| 13 | Quill left the shelf ("decided, on reflection, to redeem himself"), lost three feathers, knocked over the box of spoons, settled on the stove pipe. |
| 13 | Thursday: Mrs Fennick sits by the stove; her grate is warm. Aldevar hums a made-up fourth bar of the song ("dreadful"). She comes every Thursday since. |
| 13 | Frame, a few winters later: Aldevar lives in the stove, small and dim, still gives fire to customers with cold hands. Tansy does evening rounds with a candle and a list. Ends on the bell ringing for her coming home. |
