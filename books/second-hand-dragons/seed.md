# Seed: Second-Hand Dragons

## Premise
- Source: author's new idea (idea #6 of 10 offered on 2026-10-18)
- Starting idea: a pawnshop for retired familiars. A grumpy, half-blind dragon pawned by a wizard's widow turns out to hold the last fire the city has. Cosy comedy, warm ending.
- Logline (chosen under "go with the recommendations", 2026-10-18; "pawnbroker" changed to "shop girl" because she is the protagonist): *Pawned by a widow and furious about it, a half-blind old dragon tells how the one shop girl it couldn't scare off came to keep the city's last fire alive.*

## Protagonist
- Name / one line: Tansy, the apprentice, 16, an unredeemed pledge; and Aldevar, the dragon, the narrator
- Want / need / wound / lie: see Author said, 2026-10-18 "B + D"
- Opposition: the Midwinter auction (unredeemed pledges are sold: the dragon, and quietly the girl too) as the deadline, plus the Lamplighters' Guild as the bidder to beat or outwit (author, 2026-10-18: "3")
- Supporting roles (by function): the pawnbroker (obstacle, then ally; never get attached to stock); the widow (cost and mirror; visits on Thursdays, never redeems); the owl (comic ally; moulting, eleven years on the shelf) (author, 2026-10-18: "pawnbroker + widow + owl")
- Cast count: 5 who matter (girl, dragon, pawnbroker, widow, owl). Any Guild agent stays minor.

## Magic implications
- The power (author, 2026-10-18: "P1"): every hearth in the city was first lit from the dragon. Fire passed on freely stays warm; **fire that is sold burns cold** (light, no heat). Buying the dragon at auction can't save anyone; the fire only works if given.
- Who profits / who is hurt / who polices it (Claude's implications, offered with P1): the Guild sells light by the hour (cold); the poor pay for light that doesn't warm; the Guild holds the fire-selling charter; pawn law makes the dragon property, so its fire can never be given legally. "Borrowing a light" is a social bond; refusing one is the worst insult; a pawnshop can't take fire as a pledge.
- Cost (author, 2026-10-18: "a"): every flame the dragon gives takes a little of its remaining life; it is dying anyway, and giving brings the end closer.
- Hard limit / counter (Claude's proposal, offered with P1): fire can't be forced; stolen or bought fire goes cold. Any payment, even one coin, cools it.

## Places (2–4, with smell / sound / texture)
1. **Hobb's Pledges and Loans**, the pawnshop (front counter, the shelf, the back room): brass polish and old wool / the door bell and the ledger's pages / worn, cold counter glass.
2. **Tallow Lane**, the street and the neighbours' doors: coal smoke from the few hearths left / the hiss of Guild lamps / frost underfoot.
3. **The widow's house**: lavender and damp / a stopped clock, so silence / a cold iron grate.
4. **The auction hall**: wet coats and candle wax / the auctioneer's patter and the gavel / hard benches.
- No Guild hall scene: the Guild comes to the shop (set piece 9) and the auction.

## Subplot
- The widow (author, "yes to all"): she visits every Thursday and never redeems the dragon. She could afford to, but can't bear it at home because it smells of her husband. At the auction she has to choose.

## Set pieces (kept)

1. The dragon arrives in a coal scuttle under the widow's arm, bites the pawn ticket; told by smell.
2. ★ A customer buys a coal over the counter; it turns grey and cold in her palm.
3. ★ The girl borrows a light next door; the asking ritual, "Can you spare a light?"
4. The Guild's lamplighters on their round: bright streets, frost on the lamp glass, nobody warm.
5. The widow's Thursdays: she talks about her late husband; the dragon pretends to sleep.
6. The owl marks its eleventh year on the shelf with a speech.
7. The girl finds her ledger entry, "one girl, aged five, pledged against four crowns", and her auction lot number.
8. ★ The dragon gives its first flame, to the girl's freezing candle, and visibly dims; the shop is cold for a day.
9. A Guild agent inspects the dragon with a thermometer and a contract.
10. Night walk: the girl guides the half-blind dragon through snow to the widow's cold house (sound and smell).
11. Beside the girl's ledger entry, "not for sale" written every year in pencil and rubbed out.
12. The Midwinter auction, lots read aloud, the girl's name among them.
13. Climax: the fire passed by hand from house to house.
14. The torn pawn ticket, and the word *given*.
15. The owl finally leaves the shelf.

## Ending (chosen) and central question
- Ending (author, 2026-10-18: "E1"): the Guild wins the dragon at auction; before the handover the dragon gives its fire freely to Tansy, who carries it into the city, candle to candle, house to house, all given and all warm. The Guild is left with a creature it paid for and can't use (its fire is "bought", so it burns cold for them). The pawnbroker tears up the girl's pledge ticket and writes *given* in the ledger. Cost: the dragon spends most of its remaining life, and lives on small and dim as the shop's hearth for a few more winters, with the owl.
- Central question (Claude's, accepted under "go with the recommendations"): *Is anything worth keeping if it's only kept for its use?*
- Motifs (same): the shop-door bell, ringing at the start of every chapter; the last chapter ends on the bell ringing for someone coming home. "Can you spare a light?", said three times with a different meaning each time.
