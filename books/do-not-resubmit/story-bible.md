# Story Bible: Do Not Resubmit

## Quick Reference

**Genre:** Quiet afterlife fantasy, bureaucratic and warm, with dry humour and a happy ending.
**Logline:** An intake clerk in the Office of the dead goes looking for the evidence that will free her mother from a chair, finds that the testimony against her mother is her own, and risks her own file to set the record straight.
**Comparable Titles:** Terry Pratchett's *Going Postal* (bureaucratic magic, warmth under dryness), *The Midnight Library*, *Before the Coffee Gets Cold*.
**Target Length:** about 15k words in 7 chapters. Ch1 stays at 1,057 words; ch2–7 are about 2,300 each.
**Status:** Drafted, edited, narrated 2026-10-16 (see status.md).

**Source rule:** this book reuses chapter 1 of *The One in the Chair* word for word. Nothing else from that book applies: not its later chapters, bible, names or plot. Mira's mother is **Ruth** here.

**Author decisions (2026-10-16):** all of the proposals below are accepted. The ending must be **happy**. Ch1 is kept as written.

## Premise & Theme

### Core Premise
When people die, the Office turns them into objects according to how they are remembered. Mira, a clerk who is dead herself, has spent four years waiting for her mother's sealed case to move. Her mother is "negligent, indefinite term" and sits in the corner of Mira's flat as a chair. When Mira finally goes looking for new evidence, she finds that the heaviest testimony in the file is her own. To correct it she has to open her own file, and in it she finds how her mother remembered *her*.

### Thematic Question
Is being remembered truthfully the same thing as being remembered fairly?

### Moral Argument
A memory can be entirely true and still be unjust because of what it leaves out. Nobody can change what happened, but we can choose to remember the whole of it. When we do, the people we remember are freed, and so are we.

### Recurring Motifs
- **Warmth:** the chair's cushion is warm every evening. It goes cold in ch5 and comes back in ch7.
- **"Want to appeal?"** Mira asks the dentist in ch1 and Agnes in ch3, and finally files her own.
- **Tea:** Mira drinks too much of it, and the dentist became a teapot. In ch7 she and Ruth share a pot.
- **Sitting beside rather than in:** Mira always sits on the floor beside the chair. In ch7 she sits *with* her mother.
- **The stair light:** Agnes left it on for small Mira on the nights Ruth worked. Agnes becomes a hall lamp.
- **Stamps:** the Office speaks only in stamped phrases. *Do not resubmit without new evidence.* The last stamp is *Passage granted.*

## Plot Foundation

### Three-Act Structure

**Act I: Intake** (Chapters 1–2)
- Ordinary world: intake desk, the teapot, the flat, the warm chair.
- Inciting incident: ch2. Ruth's seasonal notice is renewed again, and the same day Mira finds Agnes Hale on tomorrow's roster.
- Protagonist's initial response: she hesitates. Then she breaks the roster rule and trades shifts with Dot to get Agnes's intake.
- Threshold crossing: the trade is logged under her name and can't be undone.

**Act II-A: Witness** (Chapter 3)
- New world / learning the rules: how testimony is taken, weighed and filed.
- Rising complications: Agnes's memory of Ruth is kind but small: the stair light, a borrowed coat. The resubmission is rejected.
- Building toward midpoint: Pell admits one testimony carries the case and warns Mira off.

**Midpoint** (Chapter 4)
- What happens: with the clock's help, Mira reads the sealed file in the Archive. The heaviest testimony is hers. She also sees a drawer with her own name on it, sealed, which has been filling since before she died.
- How it changes everything: the enemy isn't the Office but her own memory. The goal changes from finding evidence to correcting herself.

**Act II-B: The Cold Chair** (Chapter 5)
- Stakes raised: Pell spells out the cost. Amending her testimony is a personal appeal, so her own file is opened and weighed, and she could end up a doormat.
- Stripping away support: the cushion is cold and Mira can't face the chair.
- All is lost moment: every word of her testimony is true and can't be withdrawn.
- Turn: she finds the memories she left out, starting with the warm lap, and files the appeal under her own name.

**Act III: The Hearing / Passage** (Chapters 6–7)
- Final confrontation: the hearing. Pell, as the Office's advocate, reads her testimony aloud and she adds to it, one memory at a time.
- Climax: Mira's own file is opened in the same sitting. Its heaviest testimony is Ruth's, made of the memories Ruth brought with her four years ago, including the nights she stood in Mira's doorway before going to work. Ruth's term is ended, and both of them are granted **passage**.
- Resolution: in the flat, the chair becomes Ruth again. Tea. The two of them walk out of the sideways city together.

|---------|-------|--------|

### Subplots
1. **Pell's protection:** he sealed the case and had the chair placed in Mira's flat to protect her, not to block her. Opening it would cost her deferral, and he has watched clerks lose that gamble. He knew what was in Ruth's file but never read Mira's drawer, because no one may read the file of someone still working. At the end he stamps the passage himself.
2. **Dot and the shift trade (light):** Dot covers for Mira in ch2, plants the idea of "passage" as Office gossip, and turns up at the hearing in ch6.

## Character Registry

### Protagonist: Mira

**Basics**
- Age: died at about thirty, three years ago. Cause: one line at most. Proposal: a fall on icy steps.
- Occupation: intake clerk at the Office for about three years.
- Physical: not described in ch1. Keep it light.

**Psychology**
- Want: get her mother's case reopened and the indefinite term ended.
- Need: remember her mother whole, not just the absences.
- Wound: a childhood of waiting for a mother who worked nights. Ruth was a ward nurse on the night shift and slept through Mira's afternoons.
- Lie: "I'm the one who remembers her fairly; the Office got it wrong."
- Arc: positive. She goes from blaming the institution to owning her part in the record, and finds she was loved the whole time.

**Voice Notes**
- Dry, tired, procedural. Uses Office language ("intake", "placement", "file") without thinking about it.
- Kindness shows up as attention, not softness (ch1).
- She notices objects and what people were before them.

**Key Relationships**
- Ruth (the chair): she loves her, resents her, and visits every night.
- Pell: she sees him as an obstacle, and he turns out to be her protector.
- Dot: a colleague she takes for granted.

---

### Antagonist: The Office, embodied by Mr Pell

**Basics**
- Senior Clerk of Appeals, the "pay grade above" from ch1. He has been at the Office longer than anyone remembers.

**Psychology**
- Goal: keep the Office's rules intact, and keep Mira working, which means unprocessed.
- Motivation: he sealed the case under the kin rule, and because opening it would cost Mira her deferral.
- Method: delay, sealing, polite refusal. At the hearing he does his duty and reads her testimony aloud, word for word.
- Why he's a worthy opponent: he's right about the risk, and the rules he enforces are fair on their own terms.

---

### Supporting Cast

**Ruth (the chair)**: Mira's mother, a ward nurse on the night shift.
- Dark wood, a cushion worn thin, arms smooth where hands rested. As a chair she never speaks. She creaks, and the cushion is warm.
- Rare among the dead, she **appealed** at her own intake four years ago.
- She regains her shape in ch7: tired eyes, a cardigan, hands that know where to rest.

**Agnes Hale**: the witness (ch2–3).
- The neighbour from the flat below Mira's childhood home. She remembers Ruth through small things: Ruth asked her to leave the stair light on for Mira on work nights, and lent her a coat one winter.
- Becomes a hall lamp. She doesn't appeal, and is happy with it.

**The mantel clock**: guide to the Archive (ch2–4).
- Processed in ch1 ("hummed faintly when it was pleased"). In life it was an Archive clerk who finally retired.
- It can't speak. It hums when Mira is getting close to the right drawer and ticks louder when she isn't.
- Mira keeps it off the porters' list for a few days. Pet the Dog: she later makes sure it's placed somewhere it can hear the Archive.

**Dot**: fellow intake clerk.
- Swaps shifts with Mira in ch2. Cheerful, nosy, a source of Office gossip, including the story of passage. Small part.

## World Details

### Setting
**Primary Location:** the Office, and the city "slightly sideways from the one the living used".
**Time Period:** timeless, with modern trappings (dentists, cardigans, phone numbers).
**Key Locations (3):**
- **Intake (Mira's desk):** smell of paper and cold tea. Sound: the stamp. Texture: thin folders.
- **The Archive:** smell of dust and cold stone. Sound: drawers sliding, the clock humming. Texture: brass file tags.
- **Mira's flat:** smell of tea. Sound: the chair creaking. Texture: the worn cushion seam with its loose thread.
- (The hearing room in ch6–7 is part of the Office and doesn't need its own signature.)

### Sensory Palette
- Sights: shelves of objects waiting for porters, stamped notices, a window with no sun, a stair light.
- Sounds: stamps, creaks, humming, ticking. Keep the soundscape small and recognisable by ear.
- Smells: tea, paper, dust.
- Textures: glaze, worn wood, cardigan wool.
- Tastes: tea, too much and gone cold, and in ch7 hot.

### Social/Political Context
- One tension: **the Office's rules versus personal grief.** Clerks are told never to handle family, and every clerk has family on a shelf somewhere.

### Rules of the World: the Office (the one magic system)
- **Source / how it works:** at death, a soul arrives in a borrowed human shape. Its file is made of *testimonies*, which are memories of it carried by others. The file is weighed and the soul is turned into the object it amounts to. The shape fades within minutes of the stamp.
- **Testimonies travel with the rememberer.** When someone dies, their memories of everyone else are filed too, including memories of people still alive. Those go into drawers that wait. Ruth's memories of Mira have been in Mira's drawer since Ruth died.
- **Who uses it:** clerks, who are themselves dead. They read files, stamp sentences and log placements. Nobody chooses the object. It is weighed.
- **Cost:** a clerk's processing is **deferred** for as long as they work. Stopping, or filing a personal appeal, opens their own file. A working clerk's own drawer is sealed, even from Pell.
- **Hard limits:** the living can't be questioned. Testimonies can't be deleted, only added to. A clerk may not handle a family member's case.
- **Counter / weakness:** an appeal with new evidence. Most appeals die in a drawer.
- **Terms:** most sentences have a term. When it ends, the object is placed and the soul moves on, quietly. "Indefinite" means no end date until a review.
- **Passage:** the rarest outcome. A soul remembered whole keeps its own shape and walks on. Dot says it happens "about once a decade". First mention ch2, shown in ch7.
- **Objects:** can't speak. They make the sounds their object would make (a creak, a hum), and some keep a trace of warmth.
- **How society treats it:** with weary acceptance. Hardly anyone appeals, because people are relieved to be told what they amounted to.
- **First on-page demonstration:** ch1 (the teapot).
- **How the climax uses it:** Mira's personal appeal adds to her testimony instead of retracting it, which is the only legal way to change the weight. Opening her file brings out Ruth's testimony about her, and the two files together tip both into passage. All of these rules are shown before ch6: adding not deleting (ch3), drawers for the living (ch4), passage (ch2).
