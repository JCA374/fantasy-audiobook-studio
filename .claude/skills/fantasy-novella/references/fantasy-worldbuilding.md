# Fantasy Worldbuilding for Short Fiction

Build only what the story touches. Every worldbuilding fact should do one of three things: create conflict, pay off later, or characterise someone. If it does none of these, it goes in the notes, not the book.

## Magic system → "Magic" section of `story-bible.md`

Use Sanderson's three laws as the checklist:

1. **Solving problems with magic is satisfying only in proportion to how well the reader understands it.** If the climax is solved by magic, the rules must be shown on the page before the climax, ideally by ch2–3 and in action, not exposition. Soft, mysterious magic is fine, but it can only *create* problems, never solve them.
2. **Limitations matter more than powers.** Fill in:
   - **Cost**: what using it takes from the user (blood, memory, years, warmth, a relationship). This is a question, not a requirement. If the author decides there is no personal cost, record that decision and don't raise it again. The constraint then has to come from somewhere else: rules, institutions, evidence, other people.
   - **Limit**: what it can't do, no matter how strong the user.
   - **Weakness**: what stops or counters it.
3. **Expand before you add.** Explore one power's implications rather than adding a second system. A novella has room for one.

Magic section template:

```markdown
## <Name of the magic>
- Source / how it works (2–3 sentences, the in-world explanation):
- Who can use it, how it's learned:
- Cost to user:
- Hard limits:
- Counter / weakness:
- How society treats it (law, religion, fear, trade):
- How it interacts with buying, selling, giving, stealing, ownership and the law:
- First on-page demonstration: ch__
- How the climax uses or subverts it:
```

This section says what the magic is. Then the `world-rules` skill turns it into numbered rules, stress-tests them
(transactions, chains, ownership and timing, money, institutions, loopholes) and builds the reveal ladder.

## Setting in a novella

- **2–4 named places.** Give each a sensory signature: one smell, one sound, one texture. Reuse it so listeners recognise the place by ear.
- **One social tension** (class, faith, empire vs. province, guild law) that the plot pushes on.
- **History in 1–2 facts**, revealed when a character needs them. Prologues and info-dumps don't survive audio; listeners can't skim.

## Names

Names matter more in audio than in print:

- **Distinct first sounds and lengths.** Kael / Kira / Kor are hard to tell apart when heard. Aim for different initial letters and different syllable counts across the main cast.
- **Pronounceable on first sight.** Avoid apostrophes and stacked consonants unless the culture really needs them. Every one of them needs a phoneme override.
- **One naming language per culture.** Keep its sound palette consistent (soft vowels vs. hard stops), so names signal where people come from.
- Add each name to `glossary.md` **with a "sounds like"** the moment it's invented.

## Clichés to avoid (unless subverted on purpose)

Chosen-one prophecy, dark lord with no motive, tavern opening, amnesiac hero, the "unlike other girls" heroine, a medieval Europe with no economy, elves and dwarves used without a twist, mentor dies at exactly the 50% mark with no other function. Use `character-archetypes` Audit mode to catch the cast-level versions.
