# Worked example: Second-Hand Dragons (revised 2026-10-20)

The full rules (1–17) and reveal ladder are in `books/second-hand-dragons/story-bible.md`, "Rules of the World".
This page is about what went wrong without them, and which stress-test question would have caught each problem.

**The magic as first written:** sold fire burns cold, given fire is warm, giving costs the dragon. Cost, limit and
counter were all filled in. The book was drafted, edited, reader-tested and narrated, and the logic still didn't hold.

| Contradiction in the first edition | Where | Stress-test question that catches it | Rule that fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mrs Hobb refuses a coal "the minute I pay you", so a loan is treated as a sale | ch2 | 1 Transactions (is pledging paying?) | 11: a pledge is a loan; redeeming is not buying |
| The auction surplus goes to Mrs Fennick in one chapter, the shop in another | ch13 | 4 Money | 12: the lender keeps the price |
| Unclear who set the reserve, and why it matters | ch5 | 4 Money, 7 Loopholes (why doesn't the widow just bid?) | 13: the reserve is Mrs Hobb's to sign; it shuts the widow out |
| The cold houses have coal, so why are they cold? | ch10 | 2 Chains, 6 Source | 7: what stops the spread is asking, not coal |
| Withdrawal by "the ticket holder" | ch12 | 3 Ownership, 5 Institutions | 16: only the lender, by forgiving the debt |
| Quill's status unclear: a pledge with a debt, or not? | ch6, ch11, ch13 | 8 Exceptions | 17: Quill was never a pledge |
| The climax seems to happen after the sale | ch11 | 3 Ownership and timing | 14: ownership passes at noon, in the magistrates' book |
| Strand's bought dragon warms the city, yet a bought flame stays cold | ch12 | 1 Transactions (gift after purchase), 2 Chains | 4 and 5: a dragon is not a flame |
| Why is Aldevar dying at all? | ch1–9 | 6 Source and upkeep | 9: a dragon is fed by a given hearth; the Fennick grate went out |

The cold reader test after the fix (chapters only) explained every rule correctly. Its new questions were all
loopholes (could the widow buy him and then give him away? could the lamplighter's cold flame be pinched out?),
and each one got a line in the text.

**Lesson:** the rules of magic were fine on their own. The holes were where the magic met the pawn law and the
money, so the stress test asks about those first.
