# 07 · Too many clipped lines of dialogue

Source: both books, every chapter. Taught by the author from reading, not from one passage: "there are too many 2 or 3 word sentences spoken by characters. Sometimes that's OK, but if it's too often it just feels lazy."

**Measured (2026-10-18):** a *clipped* line is speech whose every sentence is three words or fewer. *The One in the Chair* has 67 of 289 spoken lines clipped (23%, up to 42% in ch11). *Do Not Resubmit* has 29 of 153 (19%). Typical ones: "Yes." / "Why?" / "I know." / "No," / "Mira." / "No,".

> "It was you," … "Yes."   (ch08: earns it, a confession)
>
> "No," … "Mira." … "No,"   (ch08: three in a row)

**Why it feels lazy:** a clipped line hands the scene over without adding anything only this character would say. One lands hard. Many in a row sound like the characters have run out of words, and with a single narrator voice they all sound alike. The emotion-beat rule (case 05) made this worse in one way: it fixes a short line by adding a beat, but a beat doesn't make "Yes." any less empty.

**Rule (speech-flow *Clipped dialogue*, writing-for-the-ear *Dialogue*, fantasy-novella drafting step 6):** about one clipped line in seven, and no two in a row unless the scene is meant to snap. Keep the ones that are decisions, confessions, interruptions or punchlines. Rewrite filler: say what they'd really say, fold the exchange into narration, or merge the beat into the line.

**Scanner:** `clipped exchange (N terse lines in a row)` flags two or more consecutive clipped lines. `clipped-dialogue share` flags a chapter (shown as ¶0) where more than 15% of its spoken lines are clipped (with at least 12 lines), and lists every clipped ¶. On the current books it flags 6 chapters of book 1 and 5 of book 2.

**Status:** the rule applies to new drafting and to the next speech-flow pass. The finished books haven't been revised.
