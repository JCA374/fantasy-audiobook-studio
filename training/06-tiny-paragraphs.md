# 06 · Two-word paragraphs can come out garbled

Source: *The One in the Chair*, ch06 (Crane's interview). **Found by the automatic listener**, not by ear: the first case listen-check discovered.

> "…They think we're a sort of police. We're not. We're a sort of wall."
>
> "A wall."

**Heard (both Whisper models agree):** "A while." Kokoro gets two words with nothing around them and slurs *wall* toward *while*. A second flag nearby, "She sat." (heard as "Is she sat?"), transcribes cleanly when the passage is rendered on its own, so it's uncertain: worth one listen.

**Proposed fix** (not yet in the manuscript; awaiting the author's ear):
- `"A wall." She didn't make it a question.` This gives the line an emotion beat (case 05) and gives the voice context. Whisper now hears *wall*.
- "She sat." merged into the next paragraph: "She sat. He did not open a file…"

`before` = the passage exactly as it is in the audiobook; `after` = with both changes.

Status: fixed in the second edition of the book (2026-10-16): "She sat." merged into the next paragraph, and "A wall." She didn't make it a question. The general rule is still rule proposal P2.
